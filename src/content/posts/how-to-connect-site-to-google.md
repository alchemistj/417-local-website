With your pages built and your website ready, it is time to connect your site to Google so you can start getting free traffic.

This takes three steps:

- Create and set up a Google Analytics account
- Create and set up Google Search Console
- Connect your website or blog to Google Analytics and Search Console

It is a fairly straightforward process, especially after the third time you do it. We will walk through each step.

**Note:** Google changes its screens and menus regularly. The screenshots below show the screens as they looked when this walkthrough was first written, so your screens may differ. If a button or menu has moved, check Google's current documentation for Analytics and Search Console.

Let's dive in.

## How to set up Google Analytics

[Google Analytics](https://analytics.google.com/analytics/web/) is one of the best ways to track your website's traffic.

You install the Google Analytics script to find out how your site performs, which page visitors enter on, how long they stay and more. A script is a piece of code that performs a function on your website. In this case, the script tells Analytics what visitors did on your site.

To get started, go to the [Google Analytics homepage](https://analytics.google.com/analytics/web/) and click the "Set up for free" button.

![The Google Analytics homepage with the Set up for free button](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-homepage.jpg)

**Note:** You need a Google account (a Gmail address) to use Google Analytics. Create one first if you do not already have one.

After logging in, you begin creating your account. First, enter an account name. The name of your website or blog works well. Leave the other options selected and click Next.

![The account creation screen in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-create-account.jpg)

Under property setup, enter your name again and change the "Reporting time zone" and "Currency" to match your location, then click Next.

![The property setup screen in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-property-setup.jpg)

Next you set up your business information, which does not change how your site works. It is mainly reporting data for Google.

Click "Select one" under industry category and choose a category. For a personal blog, "People & Society" is appropriate. If you have a specific business or niche, choose the category that most closely matches your site.

![The industry category menu in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-industry-category.jpg)

Then choose a business size and select your best guesses about how you may use Analytics. None of this affects your visitors or your traffic. Make your selections to finish creating the account and click Create.

![The business information screen in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-business-information.jpg)

Next, agree to the terms of service and data protection terms and click "I accept."

![The terms of service agreement in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-terms-of-service.jpg)

Choose which emails you want to receive from Google and click Save.

![The email preferences screen in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-email-preferences.jpg)

After that, you should land on the "Data Streams" tab in Analytics. If not, click "Data Stream" and select "Web."

![The Data Streams tab in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-data-streams.jpg)

Enter your website URL and name your stream, then click "Create stream."

![Entering a website URL and stream name in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-create-stream.jpg)

Under "Tagging instructions," select the arrow next to "Global site tag (gtag.js)." This is where you get the code for the Analytics script.

![The Global site tag script in Google Analytics](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/analytics-global-site-tag.jpg)

Leave this tab open and move on to creating your Google Search Console account.

## How to set up Google Search Console

You have a traffic tracker with Analytics. Now you need to start building traffic to track.

You need to let Google know your website exists and is live. That process is called indexing, and Google Search Console is the tool you use to make sure Google indexes your site and shows your content to searchers.

Search Console is also a valuable source of data for improving the rankings of the pages and posts on your site. We cannot encourage you enough to get to know this tool.

If you use the same Google account you used for Analytics, setting up Search Console is simple.

Go to [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) and sign in. You can also read Google's [Search Central documentation](https://developers.google.com/search) for more on how Search works.

![The Google Search Console start screen](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-start.png)

When you log in, click "Add property."

![Adding a property in Google Search Console](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-add-property.png)

Paste your entire website URL into the "URL prefix" section and click Continue.

![The URL prefix field in Google Search Console](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-url-prefix.jpg)

If you do this after linking your site to Analytics, Google can verify ownership automatically because the accounts match.

![The ownership verified screen in Google Search Console](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-verify-ownership.png)

Next, click Sitemaps and leave that screen open.

## How to add Google Analytics and Search Console to your site

The next steps depend on the website platform you use: WordPress, Squarespace, Wix and so on.

We will show how to add Analytics and Search Console to WordPress, because one plugin is a top choice for WordPress users. If you are not on WordPress, the easiest way to learn is to search for a tutorial for your platform, such as "add Google Analytics to Squarespace" and "add Search Console to Squarespace." Most website platforms have their own step-by-step help pages.

### How to add Google Analytics and Search Console to WordPress

In WordPress, go to Plugins, click "Add new" and search for Rank Math.

![Searching for a plugin in the WordPress plugin installer](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/wordpress-add-plugin.jpg)

Click Install, then Activate.

![Activating the Rank Math SEO plugin in WordPress](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-activate-plugin.jpg)

![The Rank Math dashboard after activation](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-dashboard.jpg)

![The Rank Math setup wizard welcome screen](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-wizard.png)

The first time you use Rank Math, you are prompted to create a free account. Create one and follow the prompts to activate it. Click the activate button to connect the account.

![Activating a Rank Math account](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-activate-account.jpg)

Choose the advanced option and click "Start Wizard."

![Choosing the advanced setup mode in the Rank Math wizard](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-wizard-advanced.png)

If you already have plugins with SEO settings, Rank Math finds them and asks if you want to import those settings. Choose all that are available and click "Start Import."

![The Rank Math screen for importing SEO settings from other plugins](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-import-settings.png)

Once the settings are imported, click Continue.

![Rank Math showing SEO data being imported](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-importing-data.png)

Choose the "parent" category that best represents your website.

![Choosing a site category in the Rank Math wizard](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-site-category.png)

Depending on your top-level category, you may see additional fields. If so, choose the most relevant option in each.

![Business type and other settings in the Rank Math wizard](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-business-type.png)

The images you choose in this section may show in Google search results and Facebook shares. Click "Add or Upload" to open your Media Library, choose or upload an image that follows Rank Math's current recommendations, and click Continue.

![The logo and default social image settings in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-logo-setting.png)

Next is the Google connection. Click the "Connect Google Services" button to begin.

![The Connect Google Services button in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-connect-google.png)

Choose the Google account you used to create your Analytics and Search Console accounts.

![Choosing a Google account to connect to Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-choose-account.png)

Then click Allow on the permission requests.

![Granting Rank Math permission to access Google services](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-allow-permissions.png)

Confirm to Google that you are allowing Rank Math this access.

![Confirming access for Rank Math in Google's permission prompt](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-confirm-access.png)

Next, select or confirm the Search Console and Analytics properties you are connecting to your site, and turn on the "Install analytics code" option.

![Selecting Search Console and Analytics properties and turning on Install analytics code in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-install-analytics-code.png)

Then click "Save and Continue."

Next up is the sitemap. A sitemap is an organized list that crawlers like Google's use to discover the pages and posts on your website so they can index them and show them in search results.

If it is not already on, switch on the sitemap in Rank Math and choose what to include and exclude. When deciding, ask whether a page is useful for visitors to land on from a Google search, or whether it is only there to keep the site working.

Select or deselect content types and click "Save and Continue."

![Turning on the sitemap in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-enable-sitemap.png)

On the SEO Tweaks page, make sure "Noindex Empty Category and Tag Archives" is selected. That helps keep only pages with real content in search results.

![The SEO Tweaks screen in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-seo-tweaks.png)

Next, click "Setup Advanced Options."

![The Rank Math screen showing the site is ready with an option to set up advanced options](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-advanced-options.png)

On the Role Manager screen, you can choose which users can see Rank Math's SEO data. The default is typically fine. Scroll down and click "Save and Continue."

![The Role Manager settings in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-role-manager.png)

On the 404 Monitor screen, turn on the 404 monitor and Redirections, then click "Save and Continue."

![The 404 Monitor and Redirections settings in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-404-monitor.png)

Next up is schema. Schema markup tells Google details about your content and [is a deep topic](https://www.semrush.com/blog/schema-markup/). Marking up your content can help Google show additional information in search results, which gives you more space and more compelling previews that encourage people to click.

![Examples of schema markup in Google search results](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/schema-examples.png)

You can change these settings page by page or post by post as you publish more, but you want schema enabled with basic settings in place.

![The schema markup settings in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-schema-setup.png)

After you click "Save and Continue," you land on the Rank Math dashboard. Scroll down to "Sitemap" and click Settings.

![The Rank Math dashboard with the Sitemap settings link](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-sitemap-settings.png)

Right-click and copy the link to your sitemap.

![Copying the sitemap link in Rank Math](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/rank-math-copy-sitemap-link.png)

Go back to Search Console and click the Sitemaps tab.

![The Sitemaps tab in Google Search Console](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-sitemaps-tab.png)

Paste in the link, deleting the duplicated website address so the URL looks the way it does on Rank Math's sitemap page, and click Submit.

![Pasting the sitemap link into Google Search Console](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-paste-sitemap.png)

After a few seconds you should see a success message.

![Google Search Console confirming the sitemap was submitted](./images/how-to-connect-site-to-google/search-console-sitemap-submitted.png)

That is it. Google knows about your website, Analytics is connected and you are ready to start growing your reach.

## What to do next

Depending on what you already have in motion, here are a few ideas for after you connect your site to Google.

One element to dial in is a lead capture system. That means setting up an email marketing platform and creating a landing page and other opt-in elements. Aweber is one email platform with a free plan, so check its current plans and limits.

And if you have not already, start publishing blog posts. They are one of the safest and most useful things you can do to improve your SEO. For more small business marketing ideas, see our [small business success guide](/small-business-success/).
