It was a beautiful day, so my little family and I decided to walk the [Galloway Creek Greenway](/galloway-creek-greenway-trail-in-springfield-mo/). You should too, but that's a whole other post.

As we were enjoying the trail, we struck up a conversation with some friendly passersby who asked if we were out hunting marbles.

Say what?

Apparently, marble hunting is a thing in 417 land. I got some info from the kind stranger and started investigating so I could share it with you.

## The Basics

![A person walking along a wooded edge outdoors, the kind of spot marbles get hidden](./images/marble-hunting-springfield-mo/marble-hunting-outdoors.jpg)

So what is marble hunting? Exactly what it sounds like. You go out into the great outdoors and search for little glass spheres of joy.

You don't have to search at random. To get started, join a local marble hunting group on Facebook such as [Ozarks Marble Hunters](https://m.facebook.com/groups/1615911478694186). Check that the group is still active before you join.

Members of the group hide marbles and post clues on the Facebook page. You follow the clues to find the marbles.

## Not Your Average Marble

One of my favorite discoveries is that many of the hidden marbles are hand-made by the person hiding them. Talented glass artists in 417 land love hiding their work for you to go out and find. It's real, live treasure hunting.

Fun fact: one of the friendly strangers on the trail turned out to be the mother of one of the glass artists who makes marbles for locals to hunt. Shout out to a local glass artist's mom.

I know famous people now.

## Get Out There

There are many benefits to joining the local marble hunting fun. It's an activity people of all ages can enjoy, so grab your friends and family.

It's a great excuse to get everyone outside to explore new places and enjoy the beauty of the 417 outdoors.

It also lets you get creative. Once you get the hang of hunting, try your hand at hiding some marbles for others to find.

Good luck, and have fun.
