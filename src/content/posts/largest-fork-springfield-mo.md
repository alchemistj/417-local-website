It might sound a bit odd, but when I first came to Springfield many years ago, one of my first adventures led me to the World's Largest Fork.

I do not remember the shenanigans that brought us to that area. I do remember the odd and unexpected sight of a 35-foot fork towering over our heads.

We took a picture of our surprising discovery. That was before smartphones and Facebook, so the picture was taken with an actual camera and printed out. Who knows what pile of pictures in the attic it hides in.

It was a great sight then, and it still stands for all to see.

## A Little History of the Springfield Fork

The fork was created in the 1990s to sit in front of Churchill's Coffee on S. Glenstone Ave.

Weighing in at 11 tons and standing 35 feet tall, the fork is crafted in stainless steel.

After the restaurant behind the fork shut down, Noble and Associates, the designers of the fork, moved it to their office building in the Chesterfield Village area of Springfield.

It still stands tall on that property for the world to see. Well, at least the part of the world that knows where to look. It is a bit hidden from the main road.

## How to Discover the Fork

You would think a 35-foot-tall fork would be easy to find. Not so much.

Longtime Springfieldians remember when you could spot it easily from the main road through Chesterfield Village. As more buildings went up, the fork ended up nestled among the additions and is no longer visible from Chesterfield Street.

The address to drive to is the office building at 2215 W. Chesterfield St., Springfield, MO. That gets you to the property. Once you spot the building, pass it a bit and wind around to the parking lot and main entrance, which is actually on W Butterfield Ave.

Once you are in front of the building, the fork is hard to miss. Take all the fork selfies you like.

## Springfield, MO vs. Creede, CO's Largest Fork

The giant fork in Springfield has long been touted as the world's largest fork. But is that true?

It depends on who you ask.

When the fork was built in the 1990s, it was the largest fork on record, and Springfield held that title for almost two decades. Then, in 2012, a new fork in Creede, CO came on the scene.

The competing fork comes in at 40 feet, 5 feet longer than Springfield's. But there are a few issues in comparing the two:

- **Position:** The Creede fork lies sideways and is supported by a building, while the Springfield fork stands alone. Technically, the Springfield fork stands higher but is not as long.
- **Material:** The Springfield fork is stainless steel, a common material for making forks. The Creede fork is aluminum. Can it really be considered an actual fork?
- **Weight:** The Creede fork weighs 600 pounds. The Springfield fork weighs 11 tons. Weight has to count for something.
- **Width:** The widest part of the Creede fork is about half the width of the Springfield fork. Picture two dinner forks, one a bit longer and one much wider. Which would you call larger?

I think it is clear where my vote for World's Largest Fork lies. I also fully admit that as a Springfieldian I may be just a bit biased.

The Creede fork can maybe hold the title for World's Longest Fork. I will give them that much. But claiming World's Largest Fork is definitely up for debate. Maybe one of the forks will eventually grab the attention of the Guinness Book of World Records to settle it once and for all.

My money is on our giant fork in Springfield.
